Capital gains tax for foreign investors: India's fund houses want it scrapped to stop FPI outflows 💸

India's $900 billion asset management industry is asking the Modi government to scrap capital gains tax for foreign portfolio investors (FPIs), Bloomberg reported on Monday. Foreign funds are on track for a third straight year of selling Indian stocks, with 2026 outflows nearing a record $27 billion. Today, long-term capital gains (LTCG) on listed equity are taxed at 12.5% and short-term gains (STCG) at 20%.

Why it matters: SEBI has reportedly recommended dropping LTCG tax for foreign investors, and Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has said she will consider changes to stock taxes, without confirming which. Fund managers also want debt mutual fund gains taxed like equity instead of at slab rates. Any change would most likely come in the February 2027 Union Budget.

The timing is notable: FPIs bought ₹45,960 crore of Indian shares in the Nov 2–6 week as US tech stocks sold off, while the Nifty 50 traded flat near 24,970 at midday today. Nothing has been decided yet.
